## Why this club? (345 words, limit 500)

**WHY I'M HYPED**
Advance Tech is built around what I already spend most of my days doing: learning new technology and turning it into something people can use. I learn most when I'm building, not watching. A club full of people who would rather ship a project than talk about one is exactly where I want to be.

**WHAT I'VE ALREADY BUILT**
1. **IssueLens:** a Streamlit app that uses Gemma 4 to break down GitHub issues. It won 3rd place at a hackathon.
2. **NEXMIND:** an AI learning platform based on Stanford's AI courses, with a Claude API-powered tutor.
3. **MAYANK OS:** a 14-panel productivity app with focus mode, habit tracking and an AI command centre.
4. **CORE OS:** a learning dashboard I'm building in Next.js, TypeScript and PostgreSQL.
5. **LinkedIn automation:** a workflow I'm building with Claude that writes my daily posts, creates hook images and schedules them.

I use Claude to write code, debug and automate repetitive work. Building alone taught me a lot. Building with a team will teach me far more.

**HOW I'LL ADD VALUE**
1. **Building with AI:** I can run sessions where members go from zero to a working AI tool in one evening, using Claude as a real coding partner, not just a chatbot.
2. **Automation workshops:** I'll show members how to automate the boring parts of their work, like posting, reports and repetitive tasks.
3. **Hackathon preparation:** I've been through a live judging round. I'll help our teams with demo scripts, testing and judge Q&A.
4. **Visibility:** I edit video and run an Instagram page with 2.6k followers, where one reel reached about 1 lakh views. I can make our events and projects visible beyond campus.
5. **Consistency:** I track my learning every day with tools I built. I'll bring that habit to the club: regular sessions, not one big event a semester.

**IN ONE LINE**
I don't just want to join Advance Tech. I want to help make it the club people point to when someone asks, "Where do the builders hang out?"

## Statement of Purpose (273 words, range 200–300)

My vision is simple: make Advance Tech the place where every member builds and ships at least one real project during the year. Most students learn tech by watching tutorials. Few finish a project, and even fewer show it to anyone. I want to close that gap.

As President, I'll start Build Nights every two weeks, where small teams pick a real problem and build a working prototype in one session. Beginners will pair with experienced members, so no one gets left behind. Alongside this, I'll run short, practical learning tracks on AI tools, LLM APIs and app building, plus DSA and full-stack basics, so juniors always have a clear path forward.

I'll also form hackathon squads early and run mock judging rounds. My 3rd-place finish with IssueLens taught me that preparation often decides the result. Every semester will end with a Demo Day, where members present their work, publish it on GitHub and share it on the club's socials. Over the long term, I want to bring in alumni and industry engineers as mentors and grow our presence at inter-college events.

My leadership style is to build first. I won't ask members to try anything I haven't tried myself. I keep things organized with clear plans and honest updates, but I also keep the room open, so a first-year with a rough idea feels as welcome as a senior with a finished app.

The impact I want is clear. By the end of my term, members should leave with a portfolio, not just a certificate: projects they can show recruiters, teammates they trust, and the confidence to keep building on their own.
