## 3:30–7:00 — Part 1: Generating text with Claude

**\[3:30 — THE BAD PROMPT\]** *(type live: "write a linkedin post about AI")*

Here's what most people type: "Write a LinkedIn post about AI." And here's what you get. \[Read 2 lines.\] Generic. Full of words like "game-changer." Nobody would stop scrolling for this.

**\[4:00 — THE 4-LINE PROMPT\]** *(prompt on screen, text: "SCREENSHOT THIS")*

Now the same task with a proper brief. I use four lines. Who I am. What I want. Who it's for. How it should look.

```
I am: a B.Tech student learning AI and sharing it in public.
I want: a LinkedIn post about how I use Claude to study faster.
It's for: other students who have never used AI properly.
Format: under 150 words, a strong first line, short sentences,
no buzzwords, end with a question.
```

**\[4:30 — BEFORE / AFTER\]** *(split screen: left = bad prompt result, right = 4-line result)*

Same tool, same minute. \[Read the first line of the new post.\] The difference isn't Claude. The difference is the brief. Screenshot this prompt and swap the four lines for your own topic.

**\[5:00 — IMPROVE, DON'T RESTART\]** *(type the follow-ups live)*

Now, the first answer is a draft. Don't open a new chat. Just reply. "Make the hook shorter." "Cut the last paragraph." "Sound less like a robot, more like a 20-year-old." Each reply makes it better, because Claude remembers everything in this chat.

**\[5:30 — WRITE IN YOUR VOICE\]** *(paste 3 old posts into the chat)*

The best trick for sounding like you: give it your own writing. Paste three posts or captions you wrote, and say: "Study my style — sentence length, words I use, my tone. Then write the next post in that exact style." Put this inside a Project, and every post after this sounds like you, not like AI.

**\[6:05 — BIG DOCUMENTS\]** *(drag a PDF into the chat)*

Third use: long stuff. Drop in a PDF — notes, a book chapter, a report. Ask: "Summarise this in 5 points, then turn each point into a short post." One PDF just became five pieces of content. \[Show the result and the time it took.\] If you want something you'll keep and edit, ask Claude to make it a document — you can edit it right there and download it.

**\[6:35 — HONEST CAVEAT\]** *(text on screen: "Check facts.")*

One warning. Claude can be confidently wrong, especially on numbers, dates and recent news. For anything current, turn on web search and check the sources it gives you. Never post a statistic you haven't checked.

## 10:30–11:30 — Mistakes beginners make

**\[10:30 — MISTAKES LIST\]** *(each mistake pops up as text on screen)*

Quick list of mistakes I made, so you don't have to.

1. **One-line prompts.** Use the four lines: who you are, what you want, who it's for, how it should look.
2. **Starting a new chat every time.** Reply and improve the draft instead.
3. **Trusting facts blindly.** Check numbers and news with web search.
4. **Not using Projects.** You keep explaining yourself from zero.
5. **Quitting when Claude won't make a photo.** Use the three ways from Part 2.
6. **Posting the output raw.** Read it out loud. If it doesn't sound like you, edit it.
