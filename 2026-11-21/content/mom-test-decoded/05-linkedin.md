## Post

> If 20 people tell you your idea is great,
>
> that should worry you.
>
> I'm reading The Mom Test by Rob Fitzpatrick, and it named the mistake I keep making in my outreach:
>
> I ask people for opinions. People give opinions to be kind.
>
> "Would you use this?" → "Yes, definitely." "Is this a good idea?" → "Love it."
>
> Both answers cost them nothing. So both answers are worth nothing.
>
> The book's fix is simple. Ask about the past, not the future:
>
> → Not "Would you pay for this?" but "What did you spend on this last month?" → Not "Is my idea good?" but "How do you handle this today?" → Not "What features do you want?" but "What have you already tried?"
>
> And the part that hit hardest:
>
> A call that ends with "Let's stay in touch" didn't go well. It failed.
>
> Real interest costs something: time (a next meeting), reputation (an intro to their boss), or money (a deposit). No cost, no signal.
>
> So I'm changing how I do client outreach for Creation100x. No service list in the first message. Just 3 questions about how they get leads today, and one small ask at the end.
>
> My take: most founders don't have a product problem. They have a question problem.
>
> What's the most misleading "yes" you've ever received?
