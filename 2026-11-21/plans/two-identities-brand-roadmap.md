# Mayank — Two Identities: Brand System & Roadmap (2026–2029)

**One person, two doors.**

- **Mayank Kontey** is the entrepreneur. He makes money now through branding, video editing, design, AI creativity, sales and distribution.  
- **Mayank Choudhary (AI Prompt Mayank)** is the AI engineer. He builds a career moat through AI, AI engineering and development.

**How they connect:** AI creativity is the bridge. Kontey shows *what AI can do for a brand*. PromptMayank shows *how it works and how to build it*. One idea can feed both channels from two different angles.

---

## 1\. The Big Picture

### Why two identities

- **Different audiences.** Founders and creators want results (views, sales, brand). Engineers and recruiters want proof (code, projects, depth). One account that mixes both confuses both.  
- **Different money paths.** Kontey gives you **fast cash** through clients and services. PromptMayank gives you **long-term security** through internships, jobs or AI products.  
- **Insurance.** If the business is slow, the career path is still moving. If the job market is tough, the business is still earning.

### The flywheel

1. PromptMayank learns and builds AI tools.  
2. Kontey uses those tools to deliver faster, better client work (AI ads, editing, automation).  
3. Client results become Kontey content, which brings in more clients and money.  
4. The money funds better gear, AI subscriptions and tools, which makes better content and better builds on both sides.  
5. Real projects and real clients become PromptMayank case studies, which strengthen your resume and recruiter pull.

### Ground rules

- **Kontey \= non-technical.** Explain in plain language. Story first, tool second.  
- **PromptMayank \= technical.** Show the code, the prompt, the architecture.  
- Each bio mentions the other account **once**. No more cross-posting than that.  
- **Never post the same video on both.** Same idea, different angle.

### Production workflow

1. **Idea:** pick from the idea bank  
2. **Hook:** write 3 hooks and pick the strongest  
3. **Script:** 30–45s for reels, a bullet outline for YouTube  
4. **Shoot:** batch on Sunday  
5. **Edit:** use templates for speed  
6. **Design:** cover and carousel  
7. **Post:** at a fixed time each day  
8. **Reply:** answer comments in the first 60 minutes  
9. **Repurpose:** reel → Short → LinkedIn → X  
10. **Review:** check saves, shares and retention weekly

### Fixing engagement (currently \~0.1%)

- The first 2 seconds decide everything: put the result or the conflict first, and context after.  
- One clear idea per reel. Don't stack 3 tips.  
- End with a reason to save or share ("Save this for your next edit").  
- Carousels: slide 1 is a promise, the last slide is a CTA. Aim for saves.  
- Reply to every comment, and comment on 20 posts in your niche every day.  
- Post at fixed times so your audience learns your schedule.

## 10\. Anti-Quit Rules

You've said you start things and then stop. These rules are built for that.

- **Minimum viable day:** on a bad day, do just 1 post \+ 5 DMs \+ 1 DSA problem. Never zero.  
- **No new ideas mid-phase.** Write them in the idea bank and look at them at the next phase review.  
- **Monthly review (last Sunday):** what worked, what to cut, numbers vs targets.  
- **Public accountability:** post a monthly update in your "Building to ₹2L" series.  
- **Kill criteria:** if a series gets the lowest saves and shares for 4 straight weeks, drop it.
